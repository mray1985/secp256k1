## 0. Constants

Let \(p\) be the secp256k1 field prime and \(N\) its group order. Define

\[
\Delta := p - N.
\]

Primitive cube roots of unity (nontrivial solutions of \(t^2+t+1\equiv 0\)):

- \(\beta\in\mathbb{F}_p\) (GLV endomorphism eigenvalue on the curve),
- \(\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\) (GLV scalar eigenvalue),

with reduced companions \(\beta_2=\beta^2\bmod p\) and \(\lambda_2=\lambda^2\bmod N\).

## 1. Delta algebra

### Proposition 1.1 (Definitions)

\[
p = N + \Delta.
\]

### Proposition 1.2 (Congruences)

\[
p \equiv \Delta \pmod N, \qquad N \equiv -\Delta \pmod p.
\]

**Proof.** Immediate from \(p=N+\Delta\).

### Proposition 1.3 (Invertibility)

\[
\gcd(\Delta,N)=1, \qquad \gcd(\Delta,p)=1.
\]

**Proof (verification).** Direct computation for the secp256k1 constants (both gcd equal 1). Hence \(\Delta\) is a unit mod \(N\) and mod \(p\).

### Proposition 1.4 (Inverse transport)

\[
p^{-1} \equiv \Delta^{-1} \pmod N, \qquad N^{-1} \equiv -\Delta^{-1} \pmod p.
\]

**Proof.** From Prop. 1.2–1.3: \(p\equiv\Delta\pmod N\) and \(\Delta\) invertible \(\Rightarrow p^{-1}\equiv\Delta^{-1}\pmod N\).  
Likewise \(N\equiv-\Delta\pmod p\Rightarrow N^{-1}\equiv(-\Delta)^{-1}\equiv-\Delta^{-1}\pmod p\).

### Remark 1.5

Substituting \(p=N+\Delta\) into formulas is a change of generators for the same pair of moduli; it does not by itself identify field-side and scalar-side orbit classes.

### Proposition 1.6 (Two normalizations of the same gap)

\[
\frac{\Delta}{N} = \frac{p}{N}-1, \qquad \frac{\Delta}{p} = 1-\frac{N}{p}.
\]

Since \(N<p\),

\[
\frac{\Delta}{N} > \frac{\Delta}{p}.
\]

Exact difference:

\[
\frac{\Delta}{N}-\frac{\Delta}{p}
= \Delta\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)
= \frac{\Delta(p-N)}{Np}
= \frac{\Delta^2}{Np}.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{\Delta}{N} = \frac{\Delta}{p} + \frac{\Delta^2}{Np}.
\]

Conversion identities:

\[
\frac{\Delta}{N} = \frac{\Delta/p}{1-\Delta/p}, \qquad
\frac{\Delta}{p} = \frac{\Delta/N}{1+\Delta/N}.
\]

**Proof.** Substitute \(\Delta=p-N\) into each displayed equation; all steps are identities in \(\mathbb{Q}\).

**Reading.** Same hinge \(p-N\), opposite denominators: \(\Delta/N\) is the excess of \(p\) measured against \(N\); \(\Delta/p\) is the deficit of \(N\) measured against \(p\). Asymmetric pair, extremely close but not identical.

## 2. Primitive cube-root algebra

### Setup

Work in a commutative ring \(R\) containing an element \(t\) with

\[
t^2 + t + 1 = 0, \qquad t\neq 1
\]

(when \(1\) is defined and \(t-1\) is not a zero-divisor in the relevant quotient). Then \(t^3=1\) and \(\{1,t,t^2\}\) is the cyclic group of cube roots of unity in \(R^\times\) (when invertible).

### Proposition 2.1 (Basic identities)

\[
t^2 = -t-1, \qquad t^3=1, \qquad 1+t+t^2=0.
\]

**Proof.** First from the quadratic. Multiply \(t^2+t+1=0\) by \(t-1\): \((t^3-1)=0\) if \(t\neq 1\) in an integral domain; in \(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\) for prime \(M\), same if \(t\not\equiv 1\). The sum identity is the defining equation.

### Proposition 2.2 (Linear span)

The \(R\)-module generated by \(\{1,t,t^2\}\) collapses to \(\mathrm{span}_R\{1,t\}\) via \(t^2=-t-1\).

### Proposition 2.3 (Multiplication by \(t\))

Multiplication-by-\(t\) is an \(R\)-linear automorphism of order \(3\) on any free summand where \(t\) acts invertibly, with minimal polynomial dividing \(X^2+X+1\).

## 3. Canonical lifts

### Lemma 3.1 (Forced lift for reduced representatives)

Let \(M\ge 2\), \(t\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}\) with \(t^2+t+1\equiv 0\pmod M\), and \(t_2:=(t\cdot t)\bmod M\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}\). Then

\[
1+t+t_2 = M.
\]

**Proof.** Congruence \(\Rightarrow 1+t+t_2=kM\) for some integer \(k\ge 1\). Range: \(1\le 1+t+t_2\le 2M-1\). The only multiple of \(M\) in that interval is \(M\). Hence \(k=1\).

### Remark 3.2 (Why “natural”)

The equality \(1+\beta+\beta^2=p\) (resp. \(1+\lambda+\lambda^2=N\)) is not an extra convention: it is forced by choosing reduced representatives in \([0,M)\). Non-reduced lifts (e.g. \(t'=t-M\)) break the equality and the clean carry form.

### Proposition 3.3 (Cyclotomic sums for odd prime order \(n\))

Let \(\zeta\) be a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity mod prime \(M\) (\(n\mid(M-1)\)), and let \(S\) be the sum of the \(n\) reduced residues \(\{\zeta^k\bmod M\}_{k=0}^{n-1}\subset[0,M)\). Then \(1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{n-1}\equiv 0\pmod M\), so \(S=kM\) for some \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\). The cube case \(n=3\) forces \(k=1\) by Lemma 3.1’s range \(S\le 2M-1\). For \(n=5\), \(S\le 5(M-1)\) allows \(k\in\{1,2,3,4\}\); for \(n=7\), \(k\in\{1,\ldots,6\}\). Thus **only \(n=3\) yields a uniquely forced sum equal to exactly one modulus copy** among these small cyclotomic cases under the crude range bound \(S\le n(M-1)\). Sharper uniqueness for \(n>3\) needs extra constraints (not claimed here).

## 4. Carry Reconstruction Theorem

### 4.1 Universal modular rule

From \(t^2\equiv -t-1\pmod M\), for any \(a\),

\[
a_2 := (t^2 a)\bmod M = (-a-a_1)\bmod M, \qquad a_1:=(ta)\bmod M.
\]

Piecewise on \(s=a+a_1\in\{0,1,\ldots,2M-2\}\):

\[
a_2=
\begin{cases}
M-s & \text{if }0<s<M,\\
0 & \text{if }s=M,\\
2M-s & \text{if }M<s<2M.
\end{cases}
\]

### 4.2 Boundary exclusion (prime case)

**Lemma.** If \(M\) is prime, \(a\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\}\), and \(t\) is a nontrivial cube root of unity mod \(M\), then \(a+a_1\neq M\).

**Proof.** \(a+a_1=M\Rightarrow(1+t)a\equiv 0\pmod M\Rightarrow 1+t\equiv 0\) (since \(a\neq 0\)), so \(t\equiv-1\). But then \(t^2+t+1\equiv 1\neq 0\). Contradiction.

### 4.3 Theorem (Accepted) — Carry Reconstruction for Reduced Primitive Cube-Root Orbits

Let \(M\) be prime and \(t\) a nontrivial solution of \(t^2+t+1\equiv 0\pmod M\), with \(t_2=t^2\bmod M\) reduced. For \(a\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\}\):

\[
a_1=(t a)\bmod M,
\quad
c=\Big\lfloor\frac{a+a_1}{M}\Big\rfloor\in\{0,1\},
\quad
q=1+c\in\{1,2\},
\quad
a_2=qM-a-a_1.
\]

Then \(a_2=(t_2 a)\bmod M\) and \(a+a_1+a_2=qM\).

**Proof.** Polynomial \(\Rightarrow a_2\equiv-(a+a_1)\pmod M\). Lemma 4.2 excludes \(a+a_1=M\), so \(c\in\{0,1\}\) and the two-branch lift coincides with \(a_2=qM-a-a_1\).

**Terminology:** \(c\) = carry **bit**; \(q=1+c\) = orbit-sum **class**.

### 4.4 Secp256k1 corollaries

\[
x_1=\beta x\bmod p,\ 
c_x=\Big\lfloor\frac{x+x_1}{p}\Big\rfloor,\ 
q_x=1+c_x,\ 
x_2=q_x p-x-x_1.
\]

\[
u_1=\lambda u\bmod N,\ 
c_u=\Big\lfloor\frac{u+u_1}{N}\Big\rfloor,\ 
q_u=1+c_u,\ 
u_2=q_u N-u-u_1.
\]

### 4.5 Carry algebra (composition along the orbit)

Let \(a^{(0)}=a\), \(a^{(i+1)}=(t\,a^{(i)})\bmod M\), and define **edge carries**

\[
c_i=\Big\lfloor\frac{a^{(i)}+a^{(i+1)}}{M}\Big\rfloor\in\{0,1\}.
\]

**Proposition 4.5.1 (Period 3).** \(a^{(i+3)}=a^{(i)}\) for all \(i\) (since \(t^3=1\)).

**Proposition 4.5.2 (One orbit-sum class).** The unordered sum \(S=a^{(0)}+a^{(1)}+a^{(2)}\) is invariant under cyclic relabeling, hence

\[
q := \frac{S}{M}\in\{1,2\}
\]

is a single class for the triple. Edge carries \(c_i\) may still differ; each edge reconstructs the next vertex via \(a^{(i+2)}= (1+c_i)M - a^{(i)} - a^{(i+1)}\) (indices mod 3), consistently with the same \(\{a^{(0)},a^{(1)},a^{(2)}\}\).

**Proposition 4.5.3 (Finite carry automaton).** The map

\[
a \mapsto \bigl((t a)\bmod M,\ \lfloor(a+(ta)\bmod M)/M\rfloor\bigr)
\]

has \(a\)-orbits of length dividing \(3\). Outputting the edge carry at each step yields a period-dividing-3 automaton. No claim of a bridge to the other modulus’s automaton.

**Non-claims.** Carries do not distribute over unrelated additions \(a+b\) as a ring homomorphism; \(c(a)\) is floor-theoretic / piecewise, not \(\mathbb{Z}\)-linear.

## 5. Abstract category (bridge without \(q_x=q_u\))

### Objects

Triples \((M,t,a)\) with \(M\) prime, \(t^2+t+1\equiv 0\pmod M\), \(t\not\equiv 1\), \(a\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\}\).

### Morphisms

The order-\(3\) action \(a\mapsto ta\mapsto t^2 a\mapsto a\) in \(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\).

### Minimal axioms for Carry Reconstruction

1. \(M\) prime (or \(\gcd(1+t,M)=1\)),
2. \(t^2+t+1\equiv 0\pmod M\), \(t\not\equiv-1\) (automatic if nontrivial cube root),
3. reduced representatives in \([0,M)\),
4. output: \((a_1,c,q,a_2)\) as in §4.3.

### Non-identification

The functor forgetting the ring (\(p\) vs \(N\)) shows both secp instances are objects of the **same theory**. It does **not** supply a morphism identifying \(q_x\) with \(q_u\). That identification remains closed absent a new axiom relating \(\beta\)-orbits on curve \(x\)-coordinates to \(\lambda\)-orbits on scalars.

## 6. Signature algebra and \(\Delta\)-lifts

### Separation

- Field orbit: \(x,\beta x,\beta^2 x\) in \(\mathbb{F}_p\).
- Scalar orbit: \(u,\lambda u,\lambda^2 u\) in \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\).
- ECDSA-style: \(r=R_x\bmod N\) with affine \(R=(R_x,R_y)\) and \(0\le R_x<p\).

### Proposition 6.1 (Exact lift ambiguity via \(\Delta\))

Let \(0\le R_x<p=N+\Delta\) and \(r=R_x\bmod N\). Then:

- If \(r\ge\Delta\), necessarily \(R_x=r\).
- If \(r<\Delta\), the candidates are \(\{r,\ r+N\}\), both lying in \([0,p)\).

**Proof.** Write \(R_x=r+kN\) with \(k\in\{0,1\}\) because \(R_x<p=N+\Delta<2N\) (since \(\Delta<N\) for secp256k1).  
If \(k=1\), then \(r+N<p\Rightarrow r<\Delta\). Contrapositively, \(r\ge\Delta\Rightarrow k=0\Rightarrow R_x=r\).

### Corollary 6.2

There is **no** unique exact lift \(r\mapsto R_x\) expressible from \(r\) and \(\Delta\) alone when \(r<\Delta\). Any such unique lift requires an extra bit (or equivalent information). This closes “symbolic unique \(\Delta\)-only lift” as a theorem of impossibility in that range, not as a failed correlation.

## 7. Invariants (derived candidates)

| Candidate | Status |
|-----------|--------|
| Minimal polynomial \(X^2+X+1\) | Structural generator |
| Norm/trace in \(\mathbb{Z}[t]/(t^2+t+1)\) | Trace \(-1\), norm \(1\) for multiplication-by-\(t\) on the basis \(\{1,t\}\) |
| Orbit sum class \(q=1+c\) | Invariant of the ordered triple up to the carry presentation |
| \(\Delta=p-N\) | Unit mod \(p\) and mod \(N\); transports inverses (Prop. 1.4) |
| Discriminant of \(X^2+X+1\): \(-3\) | Explains cubic residues / when roots exist (mod \(p\): \(p\equiv 1\pmod 3\)) |

**Lattice remark.** Viewing \((1,t)\) as a basis of the order \(\mathbb{Z}[t]/(t^2+t+1)\cong\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) (Eisenstein integers when embedded in \(\mathbb{C}\)) is the classical home of this polynomial; secp256k1 uses its reduction mod \(p\) and mod \(N\). No new numerical invariant claimed beyond this identification.

## 8. Role of \(\Delta\) beyond “difference”

1. **Change of modulus generators:** \(p=N+\Delta\) makes Props. 1.2–1.4 purely formal.
2. **Signature lift cut:** \(\Delta\) is exactly the width of the ambiguous residue window in Prop. 6.1.
3. **Does \(\Delta\) appear in carry reconstruction?** Not inside §4: carries are native to a single modulus \(M\in\{p,N\}\). Mixed formulas replacing \(p\) by \(N+\Delta\) are cosmetic rewrites, e.g.

\[
c_x=\Big\lfloor\frac{x+(\beta x\bmod p)}{N+\Delta}\Big\rfloor,
\]

and do not create a bridge to \(c_u\).

4. **Minimal polynomial of \(\Delta\) mod \(p\) or \(N\):** As an integer constant, \(\Delta\) satisfies the degree-1 equation \(X-\Delta=0\) over \(\mathbb{Z}\); reduced mod \(p\) or \(N\) it is simply a field/ring element (a unit). There is no higher-degree minimal polynomial over those prime fields beyond \(X-(\Delta\bmod M)\).
