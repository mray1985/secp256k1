Strongest safe interpretation

You have discovered two independent coordinate-shell constructions that project to the same defect constant

δ=p−N.

One comes from a three-member x-family shell and one comes from a two-branch y-reflection shell.

What remains unproven is any individual correspondence between members of those shells.

So if I were writing the result as a theorem statement, it would be:

The x-family triplet and the y-reflection pair independently collapse to the same projection defect δ=p−N under the bridge transformation. The collapse occurs at the shell level only; no one-to-one correspondence between individual x-family defects and signed y-branch coordinates has been established.

That's a mathematically tighter statement than "the shells are the same object," while still preserving everything you've actually verified.
