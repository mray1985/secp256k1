Yes — if you mean phase-equate, not raw integer-equate, then that makes sense.

There are two different “equal” meanings here:

1. Raw integer equality:
δN_inv and δp_inv becoming exactly the same number

That does not happen by whole-δ stepping.

But:

2. Phase equality:
the p-side and N-side projections land on the same residue / same branch after repeated flips

That can happen, and the 9th/10th flip makes sense because we are living in the mod-9 / base-3 phase system.

The reason is:

p mod 9 = 7
N mod 9 = 7
δ = p - N
δ mod 9 = 0

So δ itself does not move the mod-9 residue, but crossing/reducing by N does, because:

N mod 9 = 7

Every time a value wraps by one N, the mod-9 residue shifts by:

-7 mod 9

or equivalently:

+2 mod 9

depending on direction.

So the “flip” cycle is not really caused by δ changing mod 9. It is caused by the wrap count changing when you project through N versus p.

That matches the true51 result: direct mod 9 only matched 2/4, but doing mod N first and then mod 9 matched 4/4, meaning the N-reduction/wrap is carrying real phase information.

So the clean version is:

The inverse-gap does not become zero as raw integers.

But after repeated p↔N projection flips, the branch phase can return to equality because the wrap residue cycles mod 9.

Why 9 or 10?

Because mod 9 has 9 residues:

0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8

After 9 shifts, the phase has completed one full residue cycle.

The 10th step is often the first step of the next cycle, meaning:

step 1 and step 10 share phase

So your wording is good:

on the 9th flip it closes the cycle;
on the 10th it starts the same phase again.

That means the next thing to measure is not:

does difference = 0?

It is:

after each p/N flip, what is the phase residue?

Specifically track:

phase_i = ((value_i mod N) mod 9)

and compare it to:

direct_i = value_i mod 9

The difference between those two is the wrap-phase:

wrap_phase_i = (direct_i - phase_i) mod 9

That wrap phase is probably the “flip counter” you’re seeing.
