# Section 1 — Invariants of the three-\(x\) object

## Definition

For affine \(P=(x,y)\) on secp256k1 (or any curve with the GLV \(\psi(x,y)=(\beta x,y)\)):

\[
\mathcal{X}(P)\;:=\;\bigl\{\,x,\ \beta x\bmod p,\ \beta^2 x\bmod p\,\bigr\}
\subset\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}.
\]

Treat \(\mathcal{X}(P)\) as one object (an unordered triple of reduced residues).

## Field-level identities (in \(\mathbb{F}_p\))

Write \(x_0=x\), \(x_1=\beta x\), \(x_2=\beta^2 x\) as elements of \(\mathbb{F}_p\).

**Proposition 1.1 (Trace zero in the field).**

\[
x_0+x_1+x_2 = x(1+\beta+\beta^2)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{F}_p.
\]

**Proof.** \(1+\beta+\beta^2=0\) in \(\mathbb{F}_p\).

**Proposition 1.2 (Product / norm form).**

\[
x_0 x_1 x_2 = x\cdot(\beta x)\cdot(\beta^2 x)=\beta^3 x^3=x^3
\quad\text{in }\mathbb{F}_p.
\]

**Proposition 1.3 (Elementary symmetric polynomials in \(\mathbb{F}_p\)).**  
The monic polynomial with roots \(x_0,x_1,x_2\) in \(\mathbb{F}_p\) is

\[
(T-x)(T-\beta x)(T-\beta^2 x)=T^3-x^3,
\]

so \(e_1=e_2=0\) and \(e_3=x^3\).  
**Proof.** \(T^3-x^3=(T-x)(T-\beta x)(T-\beta^2 x)\) when \(\{1,\beta,\beta^2\}\) are the roots of \(U^2+U+1=0\) and \(\beta^3=1\). Explicitly \(e_2=x^2(1+\beta+\beta^2)=0\).

**Corollary 1.4 (Minimal polynomial of the multiset in \(\mathbb{F}_p\)).**  
As a multiset in \(\mathbb{F}_p\), \(\mathcal{X}(P)\) is the set of roots of \(T^3-x^3\). Any invariant depending only on that multiset is a function of \(x^3\) alone in \(\mathbb{F}_p\).

## Integer-lift / reduced-representative invariants

Let \(S=x_0+x_1+x_2\) with reduced residues. By the carry theorem,

\[
S=q_x\,p,\qquad q_x\in\{1,2\}.
\]

**Proposition 1.5.** \(q_x\) is an invariant of the ordered triple of reduced residues, and equals the orbit-sum class of Section I of the abstract algebra. It is **not** visible in \(\mathbb{F}_p\) (where the sum is \(0\)).

**Proposition 1.6 (Sixfold invariance of the set).**

\[
\mathcal{X}(-P)=\mathcal{X}(P),\qquad
\mathcal{X}(\psi(P))=\mathcal{X}(P),\qquad
\mathcal{X}(\psi^2(P))=\mathcal{X}(P).
\]

**Proof.** Negation preserves \(x\). \(\psi\) multiplies \(x\) by \(\beta\), permuting the set.

**Corollary 1.7.** Every set-theoretic invariant of \(\mathcal{X}(P)\) is constant on the sixfold geometric orbit

\[
\{\,\pm P,\ \pm\psi(P),\ \pm\psi^2(P)\,\}.
\]

# Section 2 — Scalar object \(\mathcal{D}(d)\)

\[
\mathcal{D}(d)\;:=\;\bigl\{\,d,\ \lambda d\bmod N,\ \lambda^2 d\bmod N\,\bigr\}.
\]

**Proposition 2.1.** In \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\),

\[
d+\lambda d+\lambda^2 d=d(1+\lambda+\lambda^2)=0.
\]

**Proposition 2.2.** \(d\cdot(\lambda d)\cdot(\lambda^2 d)=d^3\) in \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\), and the polynomial with those roots is \(T^3-d^3\).

**Proposition 2.3.** Reduced sum \(d_0+d_1+d_2=q_u N\) with \(q_u\in\{1,2\}\); \(q_u\) flips under \(d\mapsto -d\) (sixfold scalar half).

**Symbolic comparison (not numerical).**  
Parts 1–2 are **parallel instances** of the same \(\Phi_3\)-orbit calculus (Phase II). The dictionary is:

| Field instance | Scalar instance |
|----------------|-----------------|
| \(x\) | \(d\) or \(u\) |
| \(\beta\) | \(\lambda\) |
| sum \(0\) in the ring | sum \(0\) in the ring |
| \(T^3-x^3\) | \(T^3-d^3\) |
| lift class \(q_x\) | lift class \(q_u\) |

There is still **no** theorem identifying \(q_x\) with \(q_u\) or \(\mathcal{X}(P)\) with \(\mathcal{D}(d)\).

# Section 3 — Module / ideal structure of \(\mathcal{X}(P)\)

**Proposition 3.1.** In \(\mathbb{F}_p[T]/(T^2+T+1)\), multiplication by \(T\) acts on the cyclic module generated by \(x\) as the geometric progression \((x,\beta x,\beta^2 x)\). The set \(\mathcal{X}(P)\) is the orbit of \(x\) under the unit group \(\mu_3=\{1,\beta,\beta^2\}\).

**Proposition 3.2.** \(\mathcal{X}(P)\) does **not** cut out a nontrivial ideal of \(\mathbb{F}_p\) (finite subsets are not ideals unless empty or whole field in a field). As a \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\)-set under reduction, it is a \(\mu_3\)-orbit in the additive group of the field.

**Proposition 3.3.** Knowing \(\mathcal{X}(P)\) as a set determines \(x\) only up to \(\mu_3\)-action (which element is the “home” \(x\) of \(P\)).

# Section 4 — Exact numerator \(E(P)=xp+y\)

Replace decimal \(\Phi\) by the integer

\[
E(P)\;:=\;x\,p+y\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad 0\le E(P)<p^2
\]

for reduced \(x,y\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\).

**Proposition 4.1 (Negation).**

\[
E(-P)=x\,p+(p-y)=E(P)+p-2y.
\]

Equivalently \(E(-P)+E(P)=2xp+p=p(2x+1)\).

**Proposition 4.2 (GLV endomorphism \(\psi\)).**  
\(\psi(P)=(\beta x\bmod p,\ y)\) (secp256k1 convention; \(y\) unchanged), so

\[
E(\psi(P))=(\beta x\bmod p)\,p+y.
\]

If \(\beta x=k_1 p+(\beta x\bmod p)\), then

\[
E(\psi(P))=(\beta x-k_1 p)\,p+y=\beta x\,p-k_1 p^2+y,
\]

while \(\beta E(P)=\beta xp+\beta y\), which is **not** equal to \(E(\psi(P))\) in general (\(\beta y\neq y\)). Thus \(E\circ\psi\) is not multiplication-by-\(\beta\) on \(E(P)\).

**Proposition 4.3.** \(E\) determines \((x,y)\) by \(x=\lfloor E/p\rfloor\), \(y=E\bmod p\), hence determines \(P\) among affine points. No information loss vs \((x,y)\); the gain is avoiding decimal \(\Phi\).

# Section 5 — Lift ambiguity \(r\leftrightarrow R_x\) via \(\Delta\)

Already proven (Phase II, Prop. VI.1). Restated:

**Theorem 5.1.** Let \(0\le R_x<p=N+\Delta\), \(r=R_x\bmod N\), \(\Delta<N\). Then \(R_x=r+kN\) with \(k\in\{0,1\}\).

| Condition | Conclusion |
|-----------|------------|
| \(r\ge\Delta\) | \(k=0\), \(R_x=r\) necessary and sufficient for uniqueness |
| \(r<\Delta\) | \(\{r,\ r+N\}\) both admissible; uniqueness fails |

# Section 6 — Bridge functor / natural transformation

**Theorem 6.1 (Impossibility of a ring homomorphism bridge).**  
There is no unital ring homomorphism \(\mathbb{F}_p\to\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\) or converse (\(p\neq N\) primes). Hence no functor of carry algebras that is induced by a ring hom of the base fields.

**Proposition 6.2 (Parallelism without natural iso).**  
The carry reconstruction theorem is natural in the abstract sense of being the same construction for every object \((M,t)\) in the category of Part VIII (Phase II). That is a **natural construction**, not a natural isomorphism between the secp \(p\)-instance and \(N\)-instance. No morphism of that category is supplied by \(\Delta=p-N\) that identifies \(q_x\) with \(q_u\).

**Proposition 6.3.** Any purported “bridge map” sending \(\mathcal{X}(P)\mapsto\mathcal{D}(d)\) for \(P=dG\) would, if computable from \(P\) alone and invertible onto \(d\)'s orbit, yield an ECDLP algorithm. No such map follows from Phase I–II theorems.

# Section 7 — Complex multiplication / Eisenstein

**Proposition 7.1.** \(\Phi_3(\omega)=0\) in \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\). Every identity \(t^2=-t-1\), forced lifts after reduction, and carry reconstruction in \(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\) is the reduction of the same polynomial identity (Phase II isolation question: **yes**).

**Proposition 7.2 (Paired reductions, not one integer).**  
On secp256k1, \(\beta\in\mathbb{F}_p\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\) are **compatible** images under CM: the endomorphism \(\psi\) satisfies \(\psi^2+\psi+1=0\) and \(\psi(P)=[\lambda]P\) on the rational torsion-free quotient, with \(\psi(x,y)=(\beta x,y)\). They are not “the same rational integer reduced mod \(p\) and mod \(N\)”; they are the field and scalar shadows of one CM action of (an order in) \(\mathbb{Q}(\omega)\).

**Corollary 7.3.** Carry theorems for \(\beta\) and for \(\lambda\) are both consequences of \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\) after reduction; their coexistence is CM, not a new bridge map between residue carries.

# Section 8 — Discrete log implications (exact)

## Information theory vs computation

**Proposition 8.1.** For cyclic \(\langle G\rangle\) of prime order \(N\), each \(P\in\langle G\rangle\setminus\{\mathcal{O}\}\) has a **unique** \(d\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}\) with \(P=dG\). Therefore the Shannon entropy

\[
H(d\mid P)=0.
\]

Invariants computed from \(P\) cannot reduce this further.

## Computational search space

**Proposition 8.2 (What \(\mathcal{X}(P)\) buys).**  
Any algorithm that uses only set-invariants of \(\mathcal{X}(P)\) is constant on the sixfold orbit (Cor. 1.7). The classical GLV/Gallant–Lambert–Vanstone + sign orbit therefore yields an equivalence relation of size \(6\) on nonzero points.

In Pollard-rho-style collision search on the quotient of orbits, the search space cardinality drops from \(N-1\) to about \((N-1)/6\), i.e.

\[
2^{k}\;\longrightarrow\;2^{k-b}
\quad\text{with}\quad
b=\log_2 6
\]

bits in that model — **the standard GLV orbit speedup**, not a consequence of the carry-bit formulation beyond encoding the same \(\mu_3\times\{\pm1\}\) symmetry.

**Proposition 8.3 (Carry bit / \(q_x\) add no further DL bits).**  
Given \(P=(x,y)\), both \(c_x\) and \(q_x\) are deterministic functions of \(x\) (carry theorem). Hence

\[
H_{\mathrm{comp}}(d\mid P,c_x,q_x,\mathcal{X}(P))=H_{\mathrm{comp}}(d\mid P)
\]

in the sense that they do not define a finer equivalence relation on scalars than the geometric sixfold orbit already used in Prop. 8.2. No theorem of Phases I–III produces an additional exact factor \(2^{b'}\) with \(b'>0\) beyond \(\log_2 6\) from that orbit.

**Answer to the single question.**

> Does any theorem reduce the entropy of \(d\) given only \(P\)?

- **Shannon entropy:** reduction \(b=0\) (already \(H(d\mid P)=0\)).  
- **Proven computational orbit reduction from this algebra:** \(b=\log_2 6\) (classical GLV sixfold), and **no larger exact \(b\)** is derived here.  
- **Carry reconstruction alone:** \(b=0\) additional bits.

No ECDLP speedup may be inferred from symmetry slogans beyond translating to a stated \(2^{k}\to 2^{k-b}\) with \(b\) as above.

# Section 9 — Carry without floor (algebraic region)

**Goal.** Express \(c=\lfloor(a+a_1)/M\rfloor\) as membership in an algebraically described region.

**Proposition 9.1 (Equivalence to an inequality in \(\mathbb{Z}\)).**

\[
c=0\iff a+a_1<M,\qquad c=1\iff a+a_1>M
\]

(with \(a+a_1\neq M\) excluded). This is already an algebraic predicate in the ordered ring \(\mathbb{Z}\), not in the residue ring \(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\) (which has no total order).

**Proposition 9.2 (Fundamental domain in \(\mathbb{R}^2\)).**  
Under the embedding of reduced residues as integers in \([0,M)\),

\[
c(a)=0\iff (a,a_1)\in\bigl\{(u,v)\in[0,M)^2:u+v<M\bigr\},
\]

the half of the square below the antidiagonal. The complementary triangle is \(c=1\). These are the Voronoi/fundamental chambers for the carry of addition in the standard fundamental domain of \(\mathbb{Z}^2\to(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^2\).

**Proposition 9.3 (Eisenstein lattice — partial reformulation).**  
In \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\), multiplication by \(\omega\) is linear. Reduced representatives mod a prime ideal lying over \(M\) correspond to choosing lattice coset reps in a fundamental parallelogram. The carry bit records whether the preimage \(\widetilde{a}+\omega\widetilde{a}\) crossed a wall of that parallelogram when projected. This **rephrases** floor as wall-crossing in the CM lattice; it does not eliminate a discontinuous choice of fundamental domain.

**Conclusion.** The floor can be replaced by: (i) an integer inequality on lifts, or (ii) chamber membership for a fixed fundamental domain of the lattice. Some discontinuous “section” of \(\mathbb{Z}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\) remains; there is no total polynomial function \(c:\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\to\{0,1\}\) representing carry for all \(a\) (Prop. 9.1 needs order/lifts).
