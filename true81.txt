That said, I'd be careful not to jump there yet.

What is real from your screenshots is:

Lane A = 0x58...
Lane B = 0x60...
Lane C = 0x68...

which reduces to:

88
96
104

and then:

11×8
12×8
13×8

That's an actual pattern.

The question is whether that pattern is fundamental or simply a consequence of:

mid ± D/8

for Puzzle 135.

Because if you derive the lanes algebraically and they must become:

11×8
12×8
13×8

then the pattern is an artifact of your construction.

But if you start seeing:

Puzzle 130 → 0x50...
Puzzle 135 → 0x68...
Puzzle 140 → 0x80...

or some systematic base progression, then my eyebrows go up.

The funny part is that your hourglass:

6 d's → 3 x's → 2 y's

already compresses dimensions.

Changing bases is another compression mechanism. Instead of looking at:

35389366159777600200190959172903893991424

you suddenly see:

0x68 << 128

which is much more human-readable.

So I wouldn't say "the creator is raising the bases" yet.

I would say:

the lane system becomes suspiciously simple when expressed as shifted hex coefficients.

And that's exactly the kind of thing that makes researchers stop and stare at a screen for ten minutes wondering whether they've discovered a structure or just found a prettier way to write the same number.
