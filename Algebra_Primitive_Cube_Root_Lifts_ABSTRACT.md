# Part I — Primitive Cube-Root Ring

## I.1 Setup

Let \(R\) be a commutative ring and \(t\in R\) satisfy

\[
\Phi_3(t)=t^2+t+1=0.
\]

No reference to elliptic curves, \(\beta\), or \(\lambda\) is used in this part.

## I.2 Immediate identities

**Proposition I.1.** \(t^2=-t-1\), \(1+t+t^2=0\).

**Proposition I.2.** If \(t-1\) is not a zero-divisor (e.g. \(R\) an integral domain and \(t\neq 1\)), then \(t^3=1\).

**Proof.** \((t-1)(t^2+t+1)=t^3-1\).

**Proposition I.3.** \(\{1,t,t^2\}\) is closed under multiplication by \(t\), and \(t^2\in\mathrm{span}_R\{1,t\}\).

## I.3 Orbit of an element

Fix \(a\in R\). Write (when meaningful in a quotient)

\[
a_0:=a,\qquad a_1:=t a,\qquad a_2:=t^2 a.
\]

**Proposition I.4 (Polynomial forces the third).** In \(R\),

\[
a_2 = t^2 a = (-t-1)a = -a_1-a_0.
\]

Thus the third orbit member is an \(R\)-linear consequence of the first two; no second independent multiplication is required in \(R\).

## I.4 Passage to \(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\) with reduced representatives

Now specialize \(R=\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\) for an integer \(M\ge 2\), and choose a lift of \(t\) in \(\{0,\ldots,M-1\}\). Set \(t_2:=(t\cdot t)\bmod M\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}\).

**Lemma I.5 (Forced lift).** If \(t^2+t+1\equiv 0\pmod M\), then

\[
1+t+t_2=M.
\]

**Proof.** \(1+t+t_2=kM\) with \(k\ge 1\), and \(1\le 1+t+t_2\le 2M-1\), so \(k=1\).

## I.5 Carry reconstruction (abstract)

Let \(a\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\}\), \(a_1=(t a)\bmod M\).

**Universal rule.**

\[
a_2^{\mathrm{true}} = (t_2 a)\bmod M = (-a-a_1)\bmod M.
\]

**Piecewise lift** (\(s=a+a_1\)):

\[
a_2=
\begin{cases}
M-s,& 0<s<M,\\
0,& s=M,\\
2M-s,& M<s<2M.
\end{cases}
\]

**Lemma I.6 (Boundary exclusion).** If \(M\) is prime and \(t\not\equiv-1\pmod M\) (automatic for nontrivial cube roots), then \(s\neq M\) for all \(a\not\equiv 0\).

**Proof.** \(s=M\Rightarrow(1+t)a\equiv 0\Rightarrow 1+t\equiv 0\Rightarrow t\equiv-1\), contradicting \(\Phi_3(-1)=1\neq 0\).

**Theorem I.7 (Carry Reconstruction for Reduced Primitive Cube-Root Orbits).**  
Assume \(M\) prime and \(t\) a nontrivial root of \(\Phi_3\) mod \(M\). Define

\[
c=\Big\lfloor\frac{a+a_1}{M}\Big\rfloor\in\{0,1\},
\quad
q=1+c\in\{1,2\},
\quad
a_2=qM-a-a_1.
\]

Then \(a_2=(t_2 a)\bmod M\) and \(a+a_1+a_2=qM\).

**Proof.** From I.4–I.6: modular identity \(a_2\equiv-(a+a_1)\); equality case excluded; remaining two branches are exactly \(q=1+c\).

**Terminology.** \(c\) = carry **bit**; \(q\) = orbit-sum **class**.

**Conceptual form.** One multiplication by \(t\) in \(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\), plus one carry bit, reconstructs the full three-orbit — as a consequence of \(\Phi_3(t)=0\) alone.

## I.6 Instantiation (only now)

\[
(R,M,t)=(\mathbb{F}_p,\,p,\,\beta)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},\,N,\,\lambda)
\]

for secp256k1. These are two objects of the same theory, not an identification of their carry classes.

# Part II — Delta Module

Let \(p,N\) be the secp256k1 prime and order, \(\Delta:=p-N\).

**Proposition II.1.** \(p=N+\Delta\).

**Proposition II.2.** \(p\equiv\Delta\pmod N\), \(N\equiv-\Delta\pmod p\).

**Proposition II.3.** \(\gcd(\Delta,N)=\gcd(\Delta,p)=1\) (secp verification).

**Proposition II.4 (Inverse transport).**

\[
p^{-1}\equiv\Delta^{-1}\pmod N,
\qquad
N^{-1}\equiv-\Delta^{-1}\pmod p.
\]

**Proposition II.5 (Normalizations).**

\[
\frac{\Delta}{N}=\frac{p}{N}-1,\qquad
\frac{\Delta}{p}=1-\frac{N}{p},\qquad
\frac{\Delta}{N}>\frac{\Delta}{p},
\]

\[
\frac{\Delta}{N}-\frac{\Delta}{p}=\frac{\Delta^2}{Np},\qquad
\frac{\Delta}{N}=\frac{\Delta/p}{1-\Delta/p},\qquad
\frac{\Delta}{p}=\frac{\Delta/N}{1+\Delta/N}.
\]

**Proposition II.6 (Algebra generated by \(\{1,\Delta\}\)).**  
Over \(\mathbb{Z}\), \(\Delta\) generates a rank-1 free summand; the ring \(\mathbb{Z}[\Delta]=\mathbb{Z}\) since \(\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}\). As an element of \(\mathbb{F}_p\) or \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\), \(\Delta\) is a unit (Prop. II.3) satisfying the degree-1 equation \(X-(\Delta\bmod M)=0\). There is no nontrivial higher-degree minimal polynomial over those prime fields.

**Reading.** \(\{1,\Delta\}\) does not create a quadratic ring analogous to \(\mathbb{Z}[t]/(t^2+t+1)\); \(\Delta\) is a bridge constant between two moduli, not a second cyclotomic generator.

# Part III — Ring Homomorphism Question

**Question.** Does \(p=N+\Delta\) induce a natural ring homomorphism \(\mathbb{F}_p\to\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\) (or converse)?

**Proposition III.1 (No field homomorphism).**  
There is no unital ring homomorphism \(\mathbb{F}_p\to\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\) or \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{F}_p\) when \(p\neq N\) are distinct primes: the characteristic would have to satisfy \(\mathrm{char}=p\) and \(\mathrm{char}=N\) simultaneously.

**Proposition III.2 (Affine relation on lifts).**  
The identity \(p=N+\Delta\) is a relation among **integer** moduli. It induces congruences on integer representatives (Part II), and via CRT (since \(\gcd(p,N)=1\))

\[
\mathbb{Z}/(pN)\mathbb{Z}\;\simeq\;\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},
\]

but that isomorphism is not “\(p=N+\Delta\)” itself and does not yield a map of residue fields.

**Conclusion.** \(p=N+\Delta\) is an **affine / integer-lift** relationship, not a ring hom between \(\mathbb{F}_p\) and \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\).

# Part IV — Endomorphism / Eisenstein View

Work from \(\Phi_3(X)=X^2+X+1\) only.

**Proposition IV.1.** \(\mathbb{Z}[X]/(\Phi_3(X))\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) where \(\omega\) is a primitive complex cube root of unity (Eisenstein integers after identifying \(\omega\mapsto e^{2\pi i/3}\)).

**Proposition IV.2 (Trace and norm).** On the basis \(\{1,\omega\}\), multiplication-by-\(\omega\) has

\[
\mathrm{Tr}(\omega)=-1,\qquad \mathrm{N}(\omega)=1,
\]

and \(\mathrm{N}(a+b\omega)=a^2-ab+b^2\).

**Proposition IV.3 (Discriminant).** \(\mathrm{disc}(\Phi_3)=-3\).

**Proposition IV.4 (Existence of roots mod prime \(M\).** \(\Phi_3\) splits mod \(M\) iff \(-3\) is a square mod \(M\) iff \(M\equiv 1\pmod 3\) (for odd prime \(M\neq 3\)).

**Instantiation.** Reducing \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) modulo primes lying over \(p\) and over \(N\) (both \(\equiv 1\pmod 3\) for secp256k1) yields the actions of \(\beta\) and \(\lambda\). The GLV endomorphism ring story is the geometric shadow of this arithmetic; the carry theorem needs only the polynomial reduction, not the full endomorphism ring.

# Part V — Carry Algebra

**Proposition V.1 (Period 3 automaton).**  
The state transition \(a\mapsto (t a)\bmod M\) on \(\{1,\ldots,M-1\}\) has orbits of length dividing 3. Emitting

\[
c(a)=\Big\lfloor\frac{a+(ta\bmod M)}{M}\Big\rfloor
\]

at each step yields a deterministic finite output sequence of period dividing 3.

**Proposition V.2 (Single orbit-sum class).**  
\(S=a_0+a_1+a_2\) is independent of cyclic start, so \(q=S/M\in\{1,2\}\) is unique for the unordered triple. Edge carries \(c_i\) may differ.

**Proposition V.3 (Non-linearity).**  
\(c(-)\) is not a group homomorphism \(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\) in general: it is a floor function of a linear form, hence piecewise constant on intervals determined by the Beatty/floor partition of multiplication by \(t\).

**Composition.** Reconstructing \(a_2\) from \((a,a_1,c)\) and then \(a_0\) from \((a_1,a_2,c')\) returns the same triple; the automaton is consistent with \(t^3=1\).

# Part VI — Signature Lift (via \(\Delta\) only)

Separate: field \(x\); scalar \(u\); signature residue \(r=R_x\bmod N\) with \(0\le R_x<p\).

**Proposition VI.1.** Assume \(\Delta<N\) (true for secp256k1). Then \(R_x=r+kN\) with \(k\in\{0,1\}\).

- If \(r\ge\Delta\), necessarily \(k=0\) and \(R_x=r\) (**unique**).
- If \(r<\Delta\), both \(r\) and \(r+N\) lie in \([0,p)\) (**ambiguous**).

**Corollary VI.2.** There is no unique exact map \(r\mapsto R_x\) given only \(r\) and \(\Delta\) on the whole of \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\). Uniqueness holds precisely on the complement of the ambiguity interval \(\{0,1,\ldots,\Delta-1\}\).

# Part VII — Generalization to \(\Phi_n\)

**Why \(n=3\) is special (not merely a range heuristic).**

1. **Degree.** \(\deg\Phi_3=2=\varphi(3)\). The relation \(t^2=-t-1\) expresses \(t^2\) in \(\mathrm{span}\{1,t\}\). One product \(a_1=ta\) determines \(a_2\) in the ring (Part I.4); only the \([0,M)\) lift introduces a single carry bit.

2. **For prime \(n>3\).** \(\deg\Phi_n=\varphi(n)>2\). A root \(\zeta\) satisfies a degree-\(\varphi(n)\) equation; \(\zeta^{n-1}\) is **not** an \(R\)-linear function of \(\{1,\zeta\}\) alone. Reconstructing a full \(n\)-orbit \(\{a,\zeta a,\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}a\}\) requires \(\varphi(n)-1\) independent multiplications in the generic basis, not one mul + one carry.

3. **Sum of all \(n\)-th roots.** \(1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{n-1}=0\) in characteristic 0 / mod \(M\) when \(n\mid(M-1)\). Reduced residue sums equal \(kM\) for some \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\). For \(n=3\), Lemma I.5 forces \(k=1\). For \(n>3\), the range \(1\le S\le n(M-1)\) permits multiple \(k\); excluding them needs stronger arithmetic than reduced-representatives alone — the cube case is the unique small \(\Phi_n\) where degree 2 + forced single-modulus lift coincide.

**Where the proof breaks for \(n>3\):** Step “\(t^2=-t-1\) \(\Rightarrow\) one carry reconstructs the third” fails because there is no degree-2 elimination of all higher powers.
