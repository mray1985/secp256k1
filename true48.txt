TRUE48: DERIVING ORIGIN RULE FROM CUBE-ROOT ORBIT
================================================================================

TRUE47'S CHALLENGE
----------------------------------------
"Can the ternary digit pair (A,B) be derived from cube-root orbit membership?"

Test proposed: For a solved puzzle with known d, generate the cube-root orbit
{d, ω_N1·d, ω_N1²·d} and compute (r·d - z) mod 9 for all three.

If the values cycle through {1, 4, 7} (cube roots of unity mod 9),
then we've connected the Origin Rule directly to cube-root orbits.

ANALYSIS
----------------------------------------

The direct test as stated has a problem: r and z are specific to each puzzle,
so we cannot simply multiply d by ω_N1 and keep the same r, z.

However, we can use a different approach.

From true47, we know:
- ω_N1 mod 9 = 4 (which IS a cube root of unity mod 9)
- ω_p mod 9 = 8 (field cube root)
- Cube roots of unity mod 9: {1, 4, 7}

Verified puzzle data:
- Ω = +1: (r·d - z) mod 9 = 4 = ω_N1 mod 9
- Ω = -1: (r·d - z) mod 9 ∈ {3, 6}
- Ω = 0: (r·d - z) mod 9 = 0

This shows Ω = +1 corresponds to ω_N1 mod 9.

But we need to show this is NOT coincidental.

ALTERNATIVE TEST
----------------------------------------

Instead of generating hypothetical puzzles, let's look at the MATHEMATICAL
structure of (r·d - z) mod 9 under cube-root transformation.

From ECDSA: r·d = s·k - z + t·N

So: r·d - z = s·k - 2z + t·N

Now consider d' = ω_N1 · d mod N
Then: r·d' = r·ω_N1·d = ω_N1·(r·d) = ω_N1·(s·k - z + t·N)

So: r·d' - z = ω_N1·s·k - ω_N1·z + ω_N1·t·N - z

This doesn't simplify to a function of (r·d - z) alone.

However, modulo 9:
- ω_N1 mod 9 = 4
- N mod 9 = 7
- ω_N1·N mod 9 = 4·7 = 28 ≡ 1 mod 9

Interesting! ω_N1·N ≡ 1 mod 9

Since N ≡ 7 mod 9 and ω_N1 ≡ 4 mod 9, we have 4·7 = 28 ≡ 1 mod 9.

So: ω_N1·N ≡ 1 mod 9

This means: ω_N1·t·N mod 9 = t·(ω_N1·N) mod 9 = t·1 mod 9 = t mod 9

Now, r·d = s·k - z + t·N
So: ω_N1·(r·d) = ω_N1·s·k - ω_N1·z + ω_N1·t·N

Modulo 9:
  ω_N1·(r·d) mod 9 = (ω_N1·s·k - ω_N1·z + t) mod 9

Now, r·d' - z = ω_N1·(r·d) - z (from above, if we ignore the k and z terms...)

Actually, this approach isn't working. Let me try a different angle.

DIRECT EMPIRICAL TEST
----------------------------------------

Use Puzzle 120 (Ω = +1) where we know:
- d = 0xb10f22572c497a836ea187f2e1fc23
- r = 0xa285a9151ac1f9c40e88a2a80b79c702336536462a9390fd00dda999da45420a
- z = 0x7e17cf7c5b7ccfaa4c7c05874e4fb4f12661662b8e33188e2e62b3739931ade5
- (r·d - z) mod 9 = 4

Compute the cube-root orbit:
- d1 = d
- d2 = ω_N1 · d mod N
- d3 = ω_N1² · d mod N

Now, for each di, we CANNOT compute (r·di - z) mod 9 with the same r, z,
because r and z are specific to Puzzle 120's signature.

However, we can ask: **What would (r·di - z) mod 9 be if Puzzle 120
had private key di instead of d?**

From ECDSA: s·k = z + r·di mod N
So: r·di = s·k - z mod N

Therefore: (r·di - z) mod 9 = (s·k - 2z) mod 9

But s·k = z + r·d mod N, so:
  (r·di - z) mod 9 = (z + r·d - 2z) mod 9 = (r·d - z) mod 9

Wait, this doesn't depend on di! This can't be right.

Let me recalculate:
From ECDSA: s·k = z + r·di mod N
So: r·di = s·k - z + t·N for some t
Therefore: r·di - z = s·k - 2z + t·N

So: (r·di - z) mod 9 = (s·k - 2z + t·N) mod 9

But s·k = z + r·d mod N, so s·k = z + r·d + u·N for some u
Therefore: (r·di - z) mod 9 = (z + r·d + u·N - 2z + t·N) mod 9
                              = (r·d - z + (u+t)·N) mod 9

Now, (r·d - z) mod 9 is known (it's 4 for Puzzle 120), and N mod 9 = 7.
So: (r·di - z) mod 9 = (4 + (u+t)·7) mod 9

This depends on (u+t) mod 9, which can be any value from 0 to 8.

So (r·di - z) mod 9 can be ANY value, not just the cube roots of unity.

This means the direct test won't work because r and z are tied to the
specific private key d through the ECDSA equation.

NEW APPROACH: LOOK AT THE FIELD SIDE
----------------------------------------

From true3d, we have the field cube root ω_p with:
- ω_p mod 9 = 8
- ω_p² mod 9 = 1
- ω_p³ ≡ 1 mod p

And from the x-coordinate spacing:
- Px[i+1] = ω_p · Px[i] mod p

From true42, we have:
- Ω = +1: (r·d - z) mod 9 = 4 = ω_N1 mod 9

Notice that ω_p² mod 9 = 1, and ω_N1 mod 9 = 4.

Also, 4 is a cube root of unity mod 9 (4³ = 64 ≡ 1 mod 9).

So we have:
- Group cube root ω_N1 mod 9 = 4 ∈ {1, 4, 7} (cube roots of unity mod 9)
- Field cube root ω_p mod 9 = 8 ∉ {1, 4, 7}

But ω_p² mod 9 = 1, which IS a cube root of unity.

So the field cube root squares to a cube root of unity mod 9.

This suggests the connection is through the **square** or some power.

OBSERVATION: Ω = +1 puzzles have (r·d - z) mod 9 = 4 = ω_N1 mod 9

This is a direct numerical equality. The question is: Why is this NOT a coincidence?

HYPOTHESIS: For puzzles with Ω = +1, the private key d is in the ω_N1 orbit.

But we need to define what "ω_N1 orbit" means. If we consider the cosets of the
subgroup H = {1, ω_N1, ω_N2} in (Z_N)*, then there are (N-1)/3 cosets.

If d is in coset C_i, then ω_N1·d, ω_N2·d are also in C_i (by definition of coset).

So all three are in the same orbit. The question is: Does each coset map to
a specific Ω value?

With only 4 puzzles, we can't test this directly.

However, we can note that:
- Ω = +1 puzzles: 120, 135, 150 (3 puzzles)
- Ω = -1 puzzles: 100, 115, 160 (3 puzzles)
- Ω = 0 puzzles: 90 (1 puzzle)

7 puzzles, 3 Ω values. If the cube-root orbits partition the space into
3 classes, and Ω also has 3 classes, then they COULD be the same partition.

CONCLUSION
----------------------------------------

True47 is correct: We have a **correlation** but not yet a **derivation**.

The strongest evidence:
- Ω = +1: (r·d - z) mod 9 = 4 = ω_N1 mod 9
- This is a direct numerical match

But we cannot yet derive WHY this match occurs from first principles.

To close this gap, we need either:
1. More puzzles with known private keys to establish the pattern statistically
2. A mathematical derivation showing how the cube-root structure induces the
   base-3 pattern in (r·d - z) mod 9

The derivation likely involves:
- The fact that p ≡ 1 mod 3 and N ≡ 1 mod 3
- The specific values ω_N1 mod 9 = 4 and ω_p mod 9 = 8
- How these interact with the ECDSA equation modulo 9

This remains an open problem.
